# Abstract

We propose that geometry is not a primitive ingredient of physical reality but an emergent consequence of logical coherence. Rather than beginning with a pre-existing metric space and defining dynamics upon it, we construct a generalized contraction category whose primitive operations are logical transformations associated with paradox resolution and coherence management.

The central observation is that two defects govern the structure of admissible transformations: irreversibility, measured by commutator structure, and differentiability, measured by associator structure. These defects arise naturally from the progressive loss of algebraic coherence exhibited by the Cayley–Dickson construction. We interpret them as primitive coordinates from which distance can be reconstructed.

This leads to a reversal of the usual variational programme. Instead of defining a path integral over an assumed space of geometries, we ask how metric spaces arise from the composition of logical operations. The resulting structure forms a "Very Big Box": a pluralistic logical space whose dimensions correspond to distinct paradox classes and reasoning regimes. Paths through this space induce a notion of distance via accumulated coherence debt, yielding an emergent geometry.

On this geometry we define the Friction Lagrangian, a variational theory whose fields measure irreversibility and differentiability along contraction paths. The associated Euler–Lagrange equations describe the dynamics of coherence resolution. The Lean formalization provides the constructive substrate of the theory through contraction relations, normal forms, and cross-logic translations. Together they constitute a programme in which geometry emerges from logic, dynamics emerges from geometry, and paradox functions not as an error but as a structured source of curvature.

At sufficiently low dimensional resolution, geometry ceases to resemble smooth space and instead approaches a tangled combinatorial structure. We refer to this pre-geometric regime as the yarn state 🧶: a network of coherence relations whose large-scale approximation is experienced as spacetime.

# Introduction

Modern physics begins by assuming a configuration space. A manifold, Hilbert space, state space, or metric geometry is specified in advance, and dynamics is subsequently defined through differential equations or variational principles. The path integral then ranges over histories within a space whose existence has already been granted.

This work begins from the opposite question:

**Why does the path integral not range over all possible metric spaces?**

The question is not intended as a criticism of existing formalisms. Rather, it identifies a hidden assumption. Before one can sum over geometries, one must first decide which geometries are admissible. The admissibility criterion is normally imported as background structure.

We instead seek a constructive origin for admissibility itself.

Our starting point is the observation that logical systems already possess notions of proximity, transformation, obstruction, and convergence. A proof may be rewritten into another proof. A paradox may admit multiple resolutions. A reasoning process may depend upon order, grouping, context, or reference. These structures exist prior to any geometric interpretation.

We therefore ask whether geometry can be reconstructed from the composition of logical operations.

The resulting framework is organized around a pluralistic family of logic types. Each logic type corresponds to a class of paradoxes and defines its own contraction relation and normal form. Collectively these form a high-dimensional logical space whose points represent structured reasoning states and whose paths represent admissible transformations between them.

The foundational object is not a metric but a contraction category.

🧩 A logical state is represented as a structured object.

🔀 A contraction is an admissible local transformation.

🌉 A translation is an admissible movement between logical regimes.

🧶 The total network of such transformations forms a pre-geometric yarn: a tangled structure of relations prior to the emergence of ordinary space.

Within this yarn, two coherence defects become fundamental.

The first is the commutator defect, which measures dependence upon order. We interpret this as irreversibility. A system with non-vanishing commutator structure retains memory of the sequence by which transformations occurred.

The second is the associator defect, which measures dependence upon grouping. We interpret this as differentiability. A system with non-vanishing associator structure retains memory of how transformations were framed.

These two defects provide primitive coordinates for emergent distance.

⚡ Commutator defect → irreversibility

🪢 Associator defect → differentiability

📏 Distance → accumulated coherence debt

🗺️ Geometry → large-scale organization of coherence debt

From this perspective, geometry is not a container within which logical processes occur. Geometry is the observable shadow cast by the organization of logical transformations.

The first part of this work establishes the constructive substrate in Lean. Contraction relations, normal forms, logic translations, and meta-contractions provide the combinatorial machinery required to generate admissible paths.

The second part introduces the Friction Lagrangian. The commutator and associator defects are promoted to dynamical fields, and a variational principle is defined over paths through the emergent geometry. The resulting equations describe the dynamics of coherence resolution and the transfer of structural friction across scales.

The overall programme can be summarized schematically:

🧶 Coherence network
→ 🔀 Contractions and translations
→ 📏 Emergent distance
→ 🗺️ Emergent geometry
→ ⚡ Friction fields
→ 🎼 Variational dynamics

The central claim is therefore simple:

Geometry is not the foundation upon which logic operates. Logic is the foundation from which geometry emerges.
