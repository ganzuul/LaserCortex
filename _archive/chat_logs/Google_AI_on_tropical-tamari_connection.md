Yes, there is a deep geometric connection between the Tamari lattice and tropical geometry. The relationship is realized through tropical hyperplane arrangements and polyhedral subdivisions rather than a direct posetic isomorphism to a single "tropical lattice". [1, 2, 3] 
The connection bridges the combinatorial structure of the Tamari lattice with the piece-wise linear structure of tropical geometry. [1] 
------------------------------
## 1. Geometric Realizations via Tropical Hyperplanes
In algebraic combinatorics, the [Tamari lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamari_lattice) describes the poset structure of binary trees or triangulations of a polygon. Its geometric counterpart is the associahedron (Stasheff polytope). [3, 4, 5] 
Modern research by combinatorists like Ceballos, Padrol, and Sarmiento demonstrates that generalizations of this structure—such as the ν-Tamari lattice and m-Tamari lattice—possess clear tropical interpretations: [1, 2, 6, 7] 

* The Hasse Diagram as a Dual Graph: The [Hasse diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse_diagram) of a ν-Tamari lattice is isomorphic to the 1-skeleton (edge graph) of a polyhedral complex. [3, 8] 
* Tropical Hyperplane Arrangements: This polyhedral complex is explicitly induced by an arrangement of tropical hyperplanes. The intersections and bounded cells formed by these tropical hyperplanes cut out the exact geometry of the ν-associahedron. [1, 3, 8, 9] 

## 2. Triangulations of Product of Simplices
The connection relies heavily on the Develin–Sturmfels theorem, a fundamental result in [tropical geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_geometry) stating that regular subdivisions of a product of two simplices $\triangle_k \times \triangle_m$ correspond dually to configurations of tropical hyperplanes. [2] 

* The ν-Tamari lattice corresponds to the face restriction of a classical triangulation of a product of two simplices.
* By dualizing this regular triangulation tropically, the cells match the algebraic structure of the lattice, solving open visualization problems (such as those posed by F. Bergeron regarding m-Tamari lattices). [1, 2, 10] 

## 3. Application to Soliton Physics
A surprising bridge between these structures appears in mathematical physics via KP (Kadomtsev-Petviashvili) solitons: [11] 

* In a tropical approximation of a tree-shaped line soliton solution, the propagation waves behave piece-wise linearly.
* The transitions of these tropicalized soliton waves map out a maximal chain of a Tamari lattice, showing that tropical limits of certain wave equations naturally involve higher Tamari orders. [5, 11] 

------------------------------
## Summary of the Correspondence

| Tamari Side (Combinatorics) [1, 3, 4, 5, 8, 12, 13] | Tropical Side (Geometry) |
|---|---|
| Tamari Lattice / ν-Tamari Lattice | Edge graph (1-skeleton) of a polyhedral complex |
| Associahedron Face Poset | Cell chambers bounded by tropical hyperplanes |
| Tamari Right Rotations | Increasing flips in a tropical triangulation |
