# Chapter 2 — Conventional grounding, compressed

*The minimum of conventional MHD that Part II will substitute, chapter for
chapter. Claims here are standard literature; the tags [P]/[H]/[C] are reserved
for the theory and reappear in Part II.*

## 2.1 The solenoidal field and the vector potential (draft)

- `∇·B = 0`; `B = ∇×A`. The curl is the antisymmetrized derivative — the
  right-hand rule in differential form. Cross-reference Chapter 4, which
  claims our associator carries the same antisymmetry structure.

## 2.2 Frozen-in flux (draft)

- Alfvén's theorem: in ideal MHD, flux through a comoving surface is
  conserved; field lines are "frozen into" the fluid.
- Flux through a surface depends only on its boundary (Stokes).
- **Two distinct conservation senses** (both needed in Chapter 6): boundary-only
  (Stokes) and time-conserved (frozen-in). Our counterpart: `dcStep` is
  path-independent.

## 2.3 Ideal versus resistive (draft)

- Ideal MHD: no resistivity; no reconnection; flux conserved.
- Resistive MHD: reconnection allowed; flux dissipates into heat.
- The "resistivity" dial is the template for Chapter 8's Γ.

## 2.4 Flux surfaces, rotational transform, and three dimensions (draft)

- Nested flux surfaces; θ, ζ; ι (rotational transform), q (safety factor).
- Islands at rational ι; chaos; the breakdown of nested-surface assumptions.
- Quasisymmetry and quasi-isodynamicity as *design goals*, not synonyms for
  "good confinement."
